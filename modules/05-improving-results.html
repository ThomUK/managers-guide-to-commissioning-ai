Three ways to improve results

You need this if a tool “isn’t good enough” and you are asked to fund a fix.

The key idea

There are three levers, in rising order of cost and effort: ask better (prompting), give it the right documents (RAG), or change the model itself (fine-tuning). Try them in that order.

Each lever costs more than the one before, and takes longer to show whether it worked. Starting at the bottom means you spend the least to learn the most, and often the cheap fix is enough.

Prompting: ask better

A prompt is everything you give the model: instructions, examples of what good looks like, and the context it needs. Improving it costs nothing but time and can be tried in minutes. Many disappointing results come from a prompt that left the model guessing. The fix is to say who the answer is for, what form it should take and what to leave out, and to show one or two examples.

RAG: give it the right documents

A model knows only what was in its training text and what is in front of it. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) finds the passages in your documents most relevant to each question and places them in the context window beside the question.

It is like an open-book exam: the model reads the relevant pages at the time of asking. Update a document, and the next answer reflects it. The comparison stops there. A student can leaf through the whole book; the model sees only the pages the search step hands it, so if the search misses, the answer will too.

Fine-tuning: change the model

Fine-tuning is further training on your own examples, which changes the model’s weights. It can shift behaviour and style: a house format, a specialist tone. It is a poor way to add facts that change often, because every change means training again. It also needs a large set of good examples, which someone has to assemble and check.

What this means for your decisions

  • Most “the AI doesn’t know our stuff” problems are RAG problems, not fine-tuning problems.
  • RAG is only as good as the documents behind it; budget for keeping them clean and current.
  • Fine-tuning creates a model you must then own, host and re-do when the base model changes.
The detail your technical team handles optional

Embeddings and vector search

To find relevant passages, each passage is converted into a list of numbers, called an embedding, that represents its meaning. The question is converted in the same way, and the passages whose numbers are closest to it are retrieved.

Chunking

Documents are cut into passages before they are stored. How they are cut, by page, by section or by paragraph, has a large effect on whether the right material is found.

Evaluation sets

An evaluation set is a fixed list of realistic questions with known good answers. Running it before and after a change is how a team knows the change made things better, and not merely different.

LoRA

LoRA is a widely used, cheaper form of fine-tuning. It trains a small add-on to the model and leaves the original weights untouched, which makes it quicker to produce and easier to swap.

Combining all three

The levers are not alternatives. A mature system often uses a carefully written prompt, RAG for the facts, and occasionally a fine-tuned model for style.
