Local AI vs. cloud AI

You need this if you are asked to approve, buy or govern an AI tool.

The key idea

Every way of using AI answers four questions differently: who owns the model, who owns the GPU, how do you pay, and where does your data go.

Four arrangements

With a chat subscription you pay per person per month, and staff type into the vendor’s product. With API access your own software sends text to the vendor’s model, and you pay per token: the small pieces of text a model reads and writes. In both, the vendor owns the model and the GPU, the specialised processor a model runs on, and your data goes to the vendor.

The other two use a model with open weights: a model file you can download and run yourself. You can run it on a GPU rented by the hour from a cloud provider, or on hardware you buy. Renting sends your data to a machine you control in someone else’s data centre. Owning keeps it with you.

When hardware becomes your concern

In the top two rows, hardware is the vendor’s problem. Only the bottom two rows make it yours: you choose the machine, and you pay for it whether or not anyone is using it. Part B of this course explains what your technical team weighs up. Select the last arrangement to see the hardware ladder, which runs from a desk-sized computer to a rack server costing roughly 100 times as much.

Prices move, and include VAT

Treat every figure as a snapshot. The desk-sized computer launched in the UK at about £3,700 in October 2025 and sold for £5,250 to £6,399 in November 2026 (S30, S9, S10). One rental index for a single H100, a widely used data-centre GPU, rose from $2.00 an hour in December 2025 to $3.01 in November 2026 (S31).

All UK prices in this course include VAT at 20%, because most public bodies and healthcare providers cannot reclaim it, so that is the real cost to them. Organisations able to reclaim VAT will pay one-sixth less.

What this means for your decisions

  • Ask the four questions of any AI product put in front of you.
  • Per-seat pricing is predictable; per-token pricing scales with use; owned hardware is a fixed cost that is cheap only if it is busy.
  • The purchase price of large hardware is not the whole cost: add power, hosting and people. For a hosted eight-GPU server, published estimates are roughly £31,000 to £129,000 a year, converted from USD (S21).
The detail your technical team handles optional

Contract terms that change the data answer

“To the vendor” covers a wide range. Enterprise contracts usually state whether your data may be used for training future models, how long prompts and outputs are retained, and which region they are processed and stored in. Consumer and free versions of the same product often have weaker terms than the business version. Your information governance and procurement teams read these terms; the answer belongs in any approval.

Hybrid arrangements

The four rows are not the only options. A vendor’s model can be hosted inside your own cloud tenancy, so that the vendor still owns the model but the data stays within an environment you already govern. Some organisations also mix rows: a subscription for general staff use, API access for software they build, and a small machine on site for the most sensitive work.

How good are open models?

The largest open models, which need hardware near the top of the ladder, can generally match hosted models for some tasks, but not all. Smaller open models that fit on a desk-sized machine are generally less capable, and are often good enough for well-defined work such as summarising or drafting. This changes with every model release, so your technical team should test candidates on your own tasks.

Check your understanding

Your team runs an open-weights model on a GPU rented by the hour from a cloud provider. Who owns the GPU, and what are you paying for?
Show the answer
